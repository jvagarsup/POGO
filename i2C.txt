1) What is the maximum number of devices on an I2C bus?
	The maximum number of devices on an I²C bus is not fixed. It depends on:

	Available I²C addresses
	Bus capacitance
	Electrical loading
	1. Address Limit
	7-bit addressing: 128 possible addresses (0–127)
	Some addresses are reserved, so about 112–119 devices can theoretically be addressed.
	10-bit addressing: Supports up to 1024 addresses, though it is rarely used.
	2. Practical Limit

	In real applications, the number of devices is limited by the maximum bus capacitance of 400 pF (as specified by the I²C standard).
	
2) What are the I2C speed modes?
	Mode	Speed
	Standard Mode	100 kbps
	Fast Mode	400 kbps
	Fast Mode Plus	1 Mbps
	High-Speed Mode	3.4 Mbps
	Ultra Fast Mode	5 Mbps (write only)
	
3) Why are pull-up resistors required?

	Because I2C uses open-drain/open-collector outputs.

	Pull-up resistors:

	Keep SDA and SCL HIGH
	Allow devices to pull the lines LOW safely
	Prevent bus contention

	Typical values:

	4.7 kΩ
	10 kΩ

5) What is ACK and NACK?

	ACK (Acknowledge)

	- Receiver pulls SDA LOW during the ACK clock pulse.
    - NACK (Not Acknowledge)
    - SDA remains HIGH.
	- Indicates no acknowledgment or end of data.
	
6) Why does the master send NACK after the last byte?

	To indicate:

	"I don't need any more data."

	Then the master sends the STOP condition.
	
7) What is Clock Stretching?
   - A slave holds the SCL line LOW to delay the master until it is ready.
   
23. Explain Arbitration.

    Arbitration Process

	Each master:

	Sends one bit.
	Simultaneously reads the SDA line.
	Compares the bit it sent with the bit present on the bus.

	If a master sends 1 but reads 0, it knows another master is forcing the line LOW.

	That master loses arbitration and immediately stops transmitting.

	The winning master continues.
